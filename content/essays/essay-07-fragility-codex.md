## The Enumeration

130 fragility domains. 10 products per domain. 1,300 failure modes.

This is not a theoretical exercise. It is a structural enumeration of every known way that enterprise decision systems fail — compiled from operational diagnostics, governance assessments, and failure post-mortems across industries.

The Fragility Codex exists because most organizations do not fail from unknown risks. They fail from known risks that were documented, discussed, and ignored. The Codex converts "known but ignored" into "known and addressed" — or, at minimum, "known and consciously accepted."

## The Decision Trilemma

At the foundation of the Codex is a constraint that cannot be engineered away:

> Any decision-making system — human, AI, or hybrid — must trade off explainability, speed, and optimality. It is impossible to maximize all three simultaneously at scale.

This is not a preference. It is a hard boundary backed by computational theory, information theory, and control theory:

- **Explainability vs. Speed**: Explaining a decision takes time. The more explainable, the slower.
- **Speed vs. Optimality**: Fast decisions use heuristics. Heuristics sacrifice optimality.
- **Optimality vs. Explainability**: Optimal solutions to complex problems are often computationally opaque.

The trilemma is adversarially amplified by intelligence and power. AI sharpens it — faster decisions are less explainable, more optimal decisions are less auditable. Power sharpens it — the more consequential the decision, the more all three dimensions are demanded simultaneously.

Every entry in the Fragility Codex traces back to an organization's failure to navigate this trilemma.

## Why AI Amplifies Fragility

AI does not create new fragility domains. It amplifies existing ones along three vectors:

**Speed amplification**: AI can execute a bad decision in milliseconds that would previously have taken weeks of committee review. The fragility exists in both cases. AI just removes the time buffer that allowed humans to catch errors.

**Scale amplification**: AI can apply a flawed heuristic across 10,000 decisions simultaneously. The fragility of the heuristic exists regardless of scale. AI just ensures that the failure affects everything at once instead of one thing at a time.

**Opacity amplification**: AI makes the decision-making process less explainable. The fragility of opaque decisions exists in human organizations too (executive intuition, political calculations). AI just makes opacity the default instead of the exception.
